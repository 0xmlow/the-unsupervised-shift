1. **The repeated building geometry makes the street look assembled from a kit.** In the left and middle images, windows, fire escapes, rooflines and shopfronts repeat with unusually consistent spacing and depth. The right image exposes the flatness of the brick facade. Make several distinct facade modules: recess windows 0.12–0.22 m, project sills 0.05–0.09 m, add lintels, and vary cornices and floor heights between buildings. Keep each building internally orderly; put the variation between buildings.

2. **The cars look like simplified background props.** Most visible in the middle image, especially the dark van on the left and green vehicle on the right. Their boxy silhouettes, opaque windows and weak wheel detail are conspicuous because the street directs attention toward them. Replace the nearest four vehicles with better models. Give glazing actual thickness, an IOR around 1.45, dark interiors with seats, and recognizable tires, wheel wells, mirrors and lights. Use automotive paint roughness around 0.2–0.35 with a separate clear coat, and vary vehicle types and parking angles.

3. **The people do not yet read as convincing occupants.** The figure in the illuminated upper window at right is a sharply outlined, stiff silhouette. The people outside the pink shop in the middle appear clustered, similarly posed and weakly integrated with the pavement. Give the window figure an asymmetric pose, move it 0.5–1 m inside, and let a curtain or frame partially obscure it. For the sidewalk group, separate silhouettes, vary orientation and clothing, and check that feet actually contact the sidewalk. One convincing person would help more than several marginal ones.

4. **The road material is too uniform at every scale.** The left foreground has an even granular sheen; the middle has broad, smeared-looking wetness; the right has conspicuous coarse texture. Build the surface from separate scales: aggregate around 0.003–0.012 m, subtle bump below roughly 0.002 m, and irregular patches spanning 0.3–3 m. Use dry asphalt roughness around 0.75–0.9 and wet areas around 0.2–0.4. Reserve roughness below 0.1 for actual puddles in shallow depressions, especially near gutters. Break up resurfacing and wear spatially rather than adding uniform noise.

5. **The sunrise lighting looks staged around the vanishing point.** In the left image, the intensely bright street end, central reflection and evenly fading buildings form an almost diagrammatic lighting arrangement. Give the sun a definite direction consistent with facade and pavement shadows, with an angular diameter near 0.53°. Introduce distance haze that increases down the street rather than a broad luminous wash. Reduce exposure until the bright sky retains some tonal structure, then adjust atmospheric scattering and sun strength separately.

6. **The night scenes are missing believable exposure balance.** In the middle and right images, large areas of architecture collapse into darkness while shop lights and decorative elements remain exceptionally clean and legible. Increase broad sky illumination modestly—try 0.5–1 stop—and reduce individual emissive materials where necessary. Use a restrained mixture of approximately 2700–3200 K interiors and 3500–4500 K street lighting. Light the pink shop through actual luminous fixtures or concealed area lights inside it; its color should fall off across the pavement and nearby surfaces, with visible occlusion.

7. **The gate artwork reads too cleanly across its supporting surface.** Most obvious at right, where the cyan contours and white flower dominate. Horizontal slats are visible, but the artwork still has the uniform clarity of a graphic pasted onto a plane. Give the shutter a real profile, roughly 0.06–0.09 m slat pitch and 0.008–0.015 m relief. Make the paint follow those surfaces and break it only at joints, dents and selected wear points. If this is painted artwork, remove emission and illuminate it externally; a painted white flower should respond to the same light as the shutter.

8. **The right camera does not communicate the stated window viewpoint.** It reads much closer to a sidewalk-height architectural view than a photograph taken from an upper window. The large foreground road and nearly level view weaken that premise. Set the camera to the actual intended window height—for a first upper floor, roughly 4–5 m—and aim downward toward the gate. Try a 40–55 mm full-frame-equivalent lens. A small, softly defocused sill or jamb could establish the viewpoint, but the height and perspective must do the main work.

9. **The fire escapes and facade fittings lack convincing construction detail.** Across all three images, the railings and platforms read as thin repeated outlines, particularly against the darker brick. Add platform thickness, support brackets, wall connections and credible stair treads. Use approximately 0.03–0.05 m main rail diameters and thinner infill bars, with small bevels that catch light. Add a few building-specific cables, drainpipes, window AC supports and patched penetrations. These should explain how the buildings function, rather than serve as scattered decoration.

10. **The curb, sidewalk and markings are too mechanically tidy.** In the left and middle images, the crosswalk bars have crisp edges and consistently fresh coverage. The sidewalks look broadly level and cleanly joined. Add subtle slab height differences of 0.003–0.012 m, joints, localized corner damage and gutter drainage. Give road paint irregular coverage and wear concentrated in wheel paths; keep it mostly matte, around 0.65–0.85 roughness when dry. In the right image, seat the manhole cover inside a visible frame and surrounding asphalt repair instead of letting it read as a dark disk.

11. **Some street furniture is visually implausible or under-resolved.** The traffic-signal supports in the left and middle images read as large, simple bars with unclear mounting hardware. At the far right, red and green appear illuminated in the same vertical signal assembly, which immediately attracts scrutiny. Verify that each visible head has a coherent phase and orientation, add proper housings and visors, and use roughly 0.2–0.3 m signal lenses. Model the support joints and mounting brackets so the structures have an understandable purpose.

12. **The small narrative props still expose their construction.** The blue-awning shop shelves in the left and middle images resemble grids of evenly spaced colored blocks. Introduce several recognizable package shapes, gaps, overlaps and different shelf depths, with an interior extending at least 2–3 m behind the glass. The middle cat is too small here to judge anatomy reliably, but its pale, nearly horizontal silhouette reads stiffly against the road. Use an asymmetric walking pose, clear leg separation, a believable tail curve and firm paw contact. Keep its body around 0.4–0.5 m long excluding the tail; improve the pose before enlarging it for visibility.

- **Replace the nearest cars with better assets.** Their silhouettes and glass will improve both street views immediately.
- **Rebalance the lighting and exposure.** Recover facade information, control the sunrise wash and make the gate’s brightness consistent with its material.
- **Repose and simplify the people.** Partially obscure the window figure and reduce the sidewalk group to a few clearly grounded, distinct silhouettes.
